Why I Still Read My Bible Before I Read Stack Overflow

A small morning order of operations that's outlasted every framework I've ever loved.

2026-09-04

On the daily habit that's stayed constant through nine years of changing tech stacks, two companies, and more late nights than I'd like to admit.

Frameworks come and go. I’ve loved and abandoned more of them than I can count on both hands — jQuery felt permanent once, and so did a few others I won’t name and embarrass myself with. The one habit that’s survived every single stack change in nine years of building software is embarrassingly low-tech: a few minutes with my Bible before I open my laptop.

It started at Babcock, half out of obligation, half out of genuine curiosity. It’s stayed because I noticed, somewhere around year three of actually doing it consistently, that the days I skip it don’t feel dramatically worse — they just feel thinner. Less grounded. More reactive to whatever Slack message or client fire shows up first.

What this isn’t

It’s not a productivity system. I’m not going to tell you it makes your code better, because I don’t think it does, directly. It’s not a networking strategy dressed up in faith language, even though I know writing about it publicly could look that way. I’m writing about it because it’s true and because “passion for the gospel” isn’t a phrase I want to just claim — I’d rather show the unglamorous, daily, easy-to-skip version of what that actually looks like.

What it’s given me, concretely

Perspective, mostly. When a client relationship goes sideways, when Hustlrs has a rough week, when a production incident eats a Saturday I’d planned to spend with my wife — starting the day reading something older and steadier than my to-do list has a way of resizing the crisis back down to its actual size. Most of what feels urgent at 9am isn’t, and I need reminding of that more often than I’d like to admit.

Nine years in

I’ve built for fintech, healthcare, education, Web3, social platforms — CTO roles, founder roles, freelance chaos in between. The stack changes every year. The order of operations for my mornings hasn’t, and honestly, I think that’s the more interesting through-line of this whole career, even if it’s the one line item that’ll never make it onto a resume.

Coffee. Bible. Then, finally, Stack Overflow.

Quick answers

What is Oluwatosin Alli's morning routine?

Coffee, a few minutes of Bible reading, then work — a habit that started at Babcock University and has stayed constant through nine years of changing tech stacks and two companies.

How does daily faith practice affect a busy founder's week?

It resets perspective before the day's fires start, making most 9am 'urgent' problems feel appropriately sized rather than catastrophic.
