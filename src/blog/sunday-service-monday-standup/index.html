Sunday Service, Monday Standup

What actually resting on a Sabbath does to a week of shipping code.

2026-04-09

A Babcock-trained habit I almost dropped when the startup grind got loud — and why picking it back up made the rest of my week better, not worse.

There was a stretch last year where I quietly stopped resting. Not dramatically — no announcement, no burnout headline. Just Saturday nights that turned into “quick” debugging sessions and Sundays that became catch-up days because the backlog didn’t care what day it was.

Babcock drilled a rhythm into me that I didn’t appreciate until I broke it: a full day, set apart, where the laptop stays closed. Call it Sabbath, call it a hard weekly reset — either way, it’s the one habit from university that I actively had to fight to keep as an adult with a company to run.

The thing I got wrong

I used to think rest was the thing you did after the important work was finished. Turns out the important work is never finished — there’s always another ticket, another client Slack message, another “quick call.” If rest waits for an empty inbox, it waits forever.

So I stopped negotiating with it. One day, set apart — not for guilt, not because I’m supposed to, but because I actually believe the instruction behind it is wiser than my calendar. Some weeks that looks like church and a long walk. Some weeks it’s just deliberately, stubbornly not opening my laptop.

What it does to Monday

Counterintuitively, the weeks where I actually rest are the weeks I ship the most. Not because rest is a productivity hack — I refuse to reduce it to that — but because a mind that’s had a real stop in it makes fewer dumb mistakes on Tuesday. Fewer typos in production configs. Fewer “wait, why did I write it this way” moments.

I’m not going to pretend this is a clean formula. Some Sundays I still think about the backlog. But I’ve come to believe the discipline of stopping is itself the point — a weekly reminder that the company, the code, the clients, none of it is actually holding the universe together. Something else is. I just get to be useful inside of that, one standup at a time.

Quick answers

How does faith shape Oluwatosin Alli's work habits?

He keeps a weekly Sabbath rest as a non-negotiable habit from his time at Babcock University, treating it as a discipline that makes the rest of his engineering week sharper, not a productivity hack in itself.

Does taking a full day off actually help productivity?

In his experience, yes indirectly — weeks with a genuine full rest day produce fewer mistakes and more focused shipping than weeks where rest gets skipped for 'one more task.'
