I slept off. That’s usually how these days end when you’re building an enterprise-grade application under the guise of an "MVP."

Today was a weird mix of technical grind and "putting myself out there"—two things that don't always like each other. I started the morning in a frantic rush, trying to kill my weekly tasks before a meeting I thought was a formal sync. It turned into a coffee chat with the CEO instead.

Everyone wants to talk AI right now, but the vision for **Estelle** actually hit home today: building an AI agent to help shy people build their personal brands.

It’s ironic, honestly.

As a dev, I’m the one who has to explain the "how" to everyone else, and that technical translation is a heavy lift. But I’m also the guy currently staring at registration forms for a webinar I’m hosting. Am I prepared? Not even close. But my better half—who is honestly the greatest and the only reason I’m even doing this—keeps pushing me to stop hiding behind the code.

### The Grind

On the technical side, **p2pdex.io** is taking more out of me than I planned. The client keeps pushing for features, and even though they paid for an MVP, my brain won't let me ship anything less than a full enterprise system.

Today’s win was finally getting the payment receipt logic sorted. I spent the tail end of the day in **TypeScript**, wrestling with the PDF generation to make sure every receipt is automated and clean. There's a specific kind of satisfaction when the types finally align and the document renders exactly how you envisioned it.

It’s solemn because the work is a lot. It’s technical because that’s just how my brain works. But I’m excited because for the first time, I’m not just building for clients; I’m building for a version of myself that’s finally ready to be seen.

Registration forms are live. PDF code is pushed. I'm tired, but we move.
